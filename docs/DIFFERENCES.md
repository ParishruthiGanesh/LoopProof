# How LoopProof differs from a conventional reuse marketplace

Plenty of reuse listing sites exist, and LoopProof does not claim to be the first, nor to
be more effective than any of them. The difference is narrower and more specific.

A conventional marketplace treats **the listing** as the product and the handoff as
something that happens off-platform. Once an item is marked gone, whatever was claimed
about its condition becomes unverifiable, and the record lives in one organization's
editable database — if it survives at all.

LoopProof treats **the completed transfer and its evidence** as the product.

### 1. Claims carry their provenance

Every specification and condition statement is labelled `owner reported`,
`documented test` or `recipient confirmed`, and that label survives into the public
record. A model's suggestion is stored as `ai_suggested_unconfirmed`, excluded from the
public evidence manifest and from the anchored digest, until a person ticks it off.

A conventional listing has one undifferentiated description field.

### 2. Eligibility is a rule, not a ranking

An item that fails a stated requirement is rejected with the blocking reason named, rather
than shown as a weaker match. A recipient who drives across town for an item that was
never going to work is worse off than one who got no match at all.

Conventional search ranks everything and lets the user sort it out.

### 3. Completion needs two parties

Neither organization can close a transfer alone. The donor records release, the recipient
records receipt or reports a problem, and only both together produce a completed transfer.
Disputes are an explicit state, not a deleted row.

Conventionally, one party marks an item "collected" and that is the end of it.

### 4. The record outlives the platform's goodwill

A digest of the public evidence and both acknowledgments sit on a shared contract. If the
evidence is later edited, the passport says so, and anyone can recompute the digest
themselves from a published canonical document without asking either organization for
permission.

Conventionally, the record is a row in the marketplace's database, readable only through
the marketplace, and editable by whoever runs it.

### 5. Impact is withheld rather than invented

Publishing a listing earns nothing. Only confirmed completed transfers count. Measured and
estimated mass are never summed into one figure. A CO₂e number appears only when every
factor behind it has a named source and version — in this build four of five do not, so no
figure is shown and the page names the gaps.

Conventionally, an impact dashboard multiplies listings by a plausible factor.

---

### What this does not amount to

None of this makes reuse verifiable in a strong sense. It does not prove an item works,
that a handoff happened, or that the same object was not registered twice. It makes the
**paper trail** of a transfer checkable by the other party and by a third party, which is
the part that currently goes missing. That is a narrow claim, and it is the one we are
making.
